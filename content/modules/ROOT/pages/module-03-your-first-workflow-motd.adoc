= Your first workflow: message of the day

== What you will do

In Integrations and Credentials, you built the two connections
automation orchestrator needs. Now you'll put them to work. Starting
from an empty canvas, you'll build a workflow with three steps:

* A *manual trigger* that asks the person running it for a note.
* A *task agent* that turns that note into properly worded text.
* An *AAP job template* step that writes the message to a real RHEL
  host.

The workflow updates the message of the day (MOTD). When someone logs
in to a Linux server, the contents of `/etc/motd` are displayed in
their terminal. It's a useful place to communicate maintenance
windows, support contacts, and other important information. Keeping
that message current across a fleet of servers can become a repetitive
administrative task.

With this workflow, you can enter a rough note, just as you might send
to a colleague:

[source,text]
----
lab web tier, patching sat 2am utc, reboot, ping platform team if issues
----

The agent turns that into a headline, a notice, and an owning team. An
Ansible Automation Platform job template then renders those three
pieces into a banner on the host, alongside information gathered
directly from the system: its release, kernel, uptime, and disk usage.

That division is the point. The agent writes the part a person would
normally write. AAP gathers the facts that need to be accurate and
would be tedious to collect manually. Neither produces the banner
alone.

====
Stuck at any point in this lab? Automation orchestrator has a
*solutions* project containing a complete, working, published copy of

== Create the workflow

. Log in to automation orchestrator at {ao_url} as
  `{ao_admin_user}` / `{ao_admin_password}`, open *Workflows* in the
  left navigation, and click *Create workflow*.

. Name the workflow `Message of the Day` and place it in
  the `default` project as shown in the image below.
+
image::module-03/01-name-and-project.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]
+
[WARNING]
====
A workflow cannot be saved without a project, and the credentials you
created in Integrations and Credentials live in `default`. A workflow
in a different project cannot see them.
====

. Select *Manual trigger*.
+
image::module-03/02-select-trigger.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]

=== Configure the trigger

A manual trigger can collect input from whoever runs the workflow. That
input is the note your agent will work from.

. The trigger step opens with *Input schema* set to *Simple*. Click
  *Add field* and fill the row in:
+
--
* *Field name*: `banner_request`
* *Type*: `String`
* *Required*: ticked
--
+
image::module-03/03-trigger-input-field.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]

. Click *Create*.

=== Add the task agent

. Click the *+* on the right edge of the *Trigger* step and choose
  *Task Agent*.
+
image::module-03/04-add-task-agent.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]

. Open the *Model* dropdown. Your integration from Integrations and
  Credentials appears as a heading with its models underneath. Choose
  *Student LLM / qwen3-235b*.
+
image::module-03/05-select-model.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]

. A *Credential* section appears reading *LLM credential not
  configured*. Click *Set up connection*.
+
image::module-03/06-set-up-connection.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]

. Open *Select a credential* and choose *Student LLM Credential*.
+
image::module-03/07-llm-credential.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]

. Type the *Prompt*:
+
[source,text]
----
You are a RHEL systems administrator writing the login banner for a
server. An engineer has given you this note:

${trigger.banner_request}

Write the banner. Give a short headline in capitals, a notice of two
or three sentences telling anyone who logs in what to expect and when,
and the name of the team to contact.

Do not invent facts about the host. The banner already shows its real
configuration, gathered from the host itself.
----
+
Leave *Tools* as `No tools selected`, then paste this into
*Response schema*:
+
[source,json]
----
{
  "type": "object",
  "required": ["headline", "notice", "owner"],
  "properties": {
    "headline": { "type": "string" },
    "notice":   { "type": "string" },
    "owner":    { "type": "string" }
  }
}
----
+
image::module-03/08-prompt-and-schema.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]

. Click *Create*.

`${trigger.banner_request}` is what pulls the note into the prompt.
`trigger` is a reserved word in automation orchestrator, so the
expression works whatever your trigger step is called, and
`banner_request` is the field you added to its input schema a moment
ago.

The *Response schema* makes the agent answer in named fields instead
of a paragraph of prose. Prose you would have to pick apart by hand.
Named fields you can point at: `headline`, `notice` and `owner` are
exactly what the next step is going to ask for.

=== Run the agent on its own

. Open the *Task Agent* step and click *Run step*. Automation
  orchestrator asks how you want to supply the input it needs. Click
  *Set mock data*.
+
image::module-03/09-run-step-mock-data.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]
+
[NOTE]
====
If you have not saved the workflow yet, clicking *Run step* only saves
it. Click *Run step* again to get the dialog above.
====
+
Running the agent once on its own gives you a real answer in the shape
your schema describes, which is what makes the fields available to
copy and paste when you wire up the next step.

. Automation orchestrator pre-fills the shape the trigger produces, so
  the key and the braces are already there. Paste this note between
  the quotes after `banner_request`, then click *Run*:
+
[source,text]
----
lab web tier, patching sat 2am utc, reboot, ping platform team if issues
----
+
image::module-03/10-mock-input.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]

. After a few seconds the *Output* panel on the right fills with the
  agent's real answer. Find the `final_answer` block, and click
  *Update* once you have finished reviewing the output.
+
image::module-03/11-agent-answer.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]

=== Add the job template step

. Click the *+* on the right edge of the *Task Agent* step, choose
  *AAP Execution*, then *Launch AAP job template*.
+
image::module-03/12-add-aap-step.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]

. Set *Integration* to `AAP`. The same *credential not configured*
  warning appears, so click *Set up connection* and choose
  *Ansible Automation Platform*.
+
image::module-03/13-aap-credential.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]

. Set *Organization* to `Default`, then open *Job template*. The list
  is generated from the live AAP environment.
+
image::module-03/14-job-template-list.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]

. Type `Linux` to filter. Four templates match, and the other three
  belong to the next module. Choose *Linux - Set Message of the Day*.
+
image::module-03/15-job-template-motd.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]

. Scroll down to *Extra variables* and type the skeleton of what you
  are going to send, leaving the three values empty:
+
[source,json]
----
{
  "motd_headline": "",
  "motd_notice": "",
  "motd_owner": ""
}
----
+
image::module-03/16-extra-vars-skeleton.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]

. Click *Create*.

=== Wire the agent's answer into the job

Now open the *Launch AAP job template* step again and look at the
*Input* panel on the left.

It has changed. Before you ran the agent it showed a description of
what a task agent *might* return. Now it shows what yours actually
did: a `result` containing a `content` containing your three fields,
each with the real value beside it.

. Hover over `headline` in the Input panel and click the copy icon
  beside it. Automation orchestrator shows you the whole expression and
  copies it to your clipboard.
+
image::module-03/17-copy-expression.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]

. Paste it between the quotes after `"motd_headline"`. Then do the same
  for `notice` into `motd_notice` and `owner` into `motd_owner`.
+
image::module-03/18-extra-vars-filled.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]

. Click *Update*.
+
Look at the shape of what you pasted:
+
[source,text]
----
${activity_a95925fb_3e9e_4cc0_b8cf_e81f9652614c.result.content.headline}
----
+
The tail of the expression, `result.content.headline`, is the
`headline` you named in the task agent's *Response schema*. `notice`
and `owner` read the same way. What you wrote in the schema is what
you are addressing here. The long `activity_...` in the middle is the
step's generated id, which is why you copy these rather than type
them.
+
[IMPORTANT]
====
The Input panel can only offer you real fields once the step that
produces them has actually run. This is the whole reason you ran the
agent on its own a few steps ago.
====

. Click *Save workflow*.

== Run the whole workflow

. Click *Run*, then *Run now*.
+
image::module-03/19-run-workflow.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]

. This time the trigger does ask for input, pre-filled from your last
  successful run. Edit the note if you like, then click *Run*.
+
image::module-03/20-trigger-input.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]

. Watch the run details panel at the bottom. The trigger finishes
  instantly, the agent takes around twenty seconds, and the AAP job
  takes about ten more. All three end *Successful*.
+
image::module-03/21-run-complete.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]
+
[TIP]
====
The *View job in AAP* link on the last row opens the actual job in
Ansible Automation Platform, with its full output, including the
finished banner printed line by line. Automation orchestrator did not
reimplement anything: it launched a job template and waited for it.
This is great for when wanting to troubleshoot an error you may have
found with one of your AAP job templates.
====

=== See it on the host

The banner is already in place. The only way to be sure of that is to
go and look at it.

. Switch to the *Terminal* tab on the Showroom and log in to one of
  the RHEL nodes.
+
[source,bash,role=execute]
----
ssh node1
----
+
The first connection asks you to confirm the host's fingerprint. Type
`yes`, then enter `{ssh_password}` at the password prompt.
+
The banner prints before the prompt does. The headline, the notice and
the owning team are the agent's words. Everything around them is
`node1` describing itself: its release, its kernel, how long it has
been up, and how full its root filesystem is. None of that reached the
LLM model, and was strictly supplied by Ansible Automation Platform's
ability to gather facts on the host.
+
image::module-03/22-motd-banner.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]
+
[TIP]
====
Type `exit` to come back to the bastion host. Log in to `node2` to see
the same changes.
====

== What you learned

* A workflow is a *trigger plus steps*. The trigger is on the canvas
  with everything else, and `${trigger.<field>}` reaches its input from
  anywhere in the workflow.
* A *response schema* turns an agent from something that writes prose
  into something the next step can consume. The field names you choose
  there are the contract.
* *Run a step before you wire what comes after it.* Until a step has
  produced real output, automation orchestrator can only guess at its
  shape, and you are left typing expressions by hand. Afterwards it
  hands them to you.
* *Say less, not more.* Three variables went to AAP. Which hosts to
  write to, what they are running and how full their disks are were
  never worth sending, because Ansible Automation Platform already
  knows all of it.
* Automation orchestrator *decides*, Ansible Automation Platform
  *executes*. The agent chose the words; a deterministic AAP job
  template put them on a real server next to facts it measured itself.
