= Your First Workflow: Vacation Anyone?

== What You Will Do

In Integrations and Credentials you built the two connections
automation orchestrator needs. Now you use them. Starting from an
empty canvas you build a workflow with three steps:

* A *manual trigger* that asks the person running it a question
* A *task agent* that answers the question with an AI model
* An *AAP job template* step that posts the answer to a chatroom

The workflow is a vacation recommender, because who doesn't need to
plan their next vacation? In this workflow when you type something
like `Somewhere warm in March, not too touristy`, an AI agent picks a
city and explains why. It then uses an existing job template within
Ansible Automation Platform to post about it in a Mattermost
chatroom.

While we love us a great vacation, what this first workflow is going
to teach you is how a trigger collects input, how a task agent takes
your input and turns it into a structured response, and how you can
leverage AAP to place those insights exactly where you want them.
Every automation orchestrator workflow you will use today will take
advantage of these different steps so that you too can take your
automation to the next level.

[TIP]
====
Stuck at any point in this lab? Automation orchestrator has a
*solutions* project containing a complete, working, published copy of
everything you are asked to build. Switch the *Project* selector to
`solutions` and compare. For this module, look at the workflow named
*Vacation Suggestion*. It is the finished version of what you are
about to create.
====

== Create the Workflow

. Log in to automation orchestrator at {ao_url} as
  `{ao_admin_user}` / `{ao_admin_password}`, open *Workflows* in the
  left navigation, and click *Create workflow*.

. Name the workflow `Vacation Suggestion` and place it in the `default`
  project as shown in the image below.
+
image::module-03/01-name-and-project.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]
+
[WARNING]
====
A workflow cannot be saved without a project, and the credentials you

====

. Select *Manual trigger*.
+
image::module-03/02-select-trigger.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]

=== Configure the Trigger

A manual trigger can collect input from whoever runs the workflow. That
input is what your agent will answer.

. The trigger step opens with *Input schema* set to *Simple*. Click
  *Add field* and fill the row in:
+
--
* *Field name*: `vacation_request`
* *Type*: `String`
* *Required*: ticked
--
+
image::module-03/03-trigger-input-field.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]

. Click *Create*.

=== Add the Task Agent

. Click the *+* on the right edge of the *Trigger* step and choose
  *Task Agent*.
+
image::module-03/04-add-task-agent.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]

. Open the *Model* dropdown. Your integration from Integrations and
  Credentials appears as a heading with its models underneath. Choose
  *Student LLM / qwen3-235b*.
+
image::module-03/05-select-model.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]

. A *Credential* section appears reading *LLM credential not
  configured*. Click *Set up connection*.
+
image::module-03/06-set-up-connection.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]

. Open *Select a credential* and choose *Student LLM Credential*.
+
image::module-03/07-llm-credential.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]

. Type the *Prompt*:
+
[source,text]
----
You are a well-travelled colleague. Someone asks you:

${trigger.vacation_request}

Recommend one city. Give the city, a short reason, and one
genuinely surprising fun fact about it.
----
+
Leave *Tools* as `No tools selected`, then paste this into
*Response schema*:
+
[source,json]
----
{
  "type": "object",
  "required": ["city", "reason", "fun_fact"],
  "properties": {
    "city":     { "type": "string" },
    "reason":   { "type": "string" },
    "fun_fact": { "type": "string" }
  }
}
----
+
image::module-03/08-prompt-and-schema.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]

. Click *Create*.

`${trigger.vacation_request}` is what pulls the question into the
prompt. `trigger` is a reserved word in automation orchestrator, so
the expression works whatever your trigger step is called, and
`vacation_request` is the field you added to its input schema a moment
ago.

The *Response schema* makes the agent answer in named fields instead
of a paragraph of prose. Prose you would have to pick apart by hand.
Named fields you can point at: `city`, `reason` and `fun_fact` are
exactly what the next step is going to ask for.

=== Run the Agent on Its Own

. Open the *Task Agent* step and click *Run step*. Automation
  orchestrator asks how you want to supply the input it needs. Click
  *Set mock data*.
+
image::module-03/09-run-step-mock-data.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]
+
Running the agent once on its own gives you a real answer in the shape
your schema describes, which is what makes the fields available to
copy and paste when you wire up the next step.

. Automation orchestrator pre-fills the shape the trigger produces.
  Fill in a question and click *Run*:
+
[source,json]
----
{
  "vacation_request": "Somewhere warm in March, not too touristy"
}
----
+
image::module-03/10-mock-input.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]

. After twenty to thirty seconds the *Output* panel on the right fills
  with the agent's real answer. Find the `final_answer` block, and
  click *Update* once you have finished reviewing the output.
+
image::module-03/11-agent-answer.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]

[NOTE]
====
Your city will not be Marrakech. That is the point: nothing downstream
can be hard-coded to a particular answer, only to the three field
names you put in the schema.
====

=== Add the Chatroom Step

. Click the *+* on the right edge of the *Task Agent* step, choose
  *AAP Execution*, then *Launch AAP job template*.
+
image::module-03/12-add-aap-step.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]

. Set *Integration* to `AAP`. The same *credential not configured*
  warning appears, so click *Set up connection* and choose
  *Ansible Automation Platform*.
+
image::module-03/13-aap-credential.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]

. Set *Organization* to `Default`, then open *Job template*. The list
  is generated from the live AAP environment.
+
image::module-03/14-job-template-list.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]

. Type `notify` to filter. Two templates match. Choose
  *Notify Chatroom*.
+
image::module-03/15-job-template-notify.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]

. Scroll down to *Extra variables* and type the skeleton of what you
  are going to send, leaving three of the values empty:
+
[source,json]
----
{
  "notify_card": "hello",
  "notify_title": "",
  "notify_text": "",
  "notify_fact": ""
}
----
+
image::module-03/16-extra-vars-skeleton.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]
+
[NOTE]
====
`notify_card` tells the playbook which notification card to build.
`hello` is this module's card: a title, a body and a single fun fact.
====

. Click *Create*.

=== Wire the Agent's Answer into the Job

Now open the *Launch AAP job template* step again and look at the
*Input* panel on the left.

It has changed. Before you ran the agent it showed a description of
what a task agent *might* return. Now it shows what yours actually
did: a `result` containing a `content` containing your three fields,
each with the real value beside it.

. Hover over `city` in the Input panel and click the copy icon beside
  it. Automation orchestrator shows you the whole expression and copies
  it to your clipboard.
+
image::module-03/17-copy-expression.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]

. Paste it between the quotes after `"notify_title"`. Then do the same
  for `reason` into `notify_text` and `fun_fact` into `notify_fact`.
+
image::module-03/18-extra-vars-filled.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]

. Click *Update*, then *Save workflow*.

Look at the shape of what you pasted:

[source,text]
----
${activity_a95925fb_3e9e_4cc0_b8cf_e81f9652614c.result.content.city}
----

The tail of the expression, `result.content.city`, is the `city` you
named in the task agent's *Response schema*. `reason` and `fun_fact`
read the same way. What you wrote in the schema is what you are
addressing here. The long `activity_...` in the middle is the step's
generated id, which is why you copy these rather than type them.

[IMPORTANT]
====
The Input panel can only offer you real fields once the step that
produces them has actually run. This is the whole reason you ran the
agent on its own a few steps ago.
====

== Run the Whole Workflow

. Click *Run*, then *Run now*.
+
image::module-03/19-run-workflow.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]

. This time the trigger does ask for input, pre-filled from your last
  successful run. Edit it if you like, then click *Run*.
+
image::module-03/20-trigger-input.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]

. Watch the run details panel at the bottom. The trigger finishes
  instantly, the agent takes around twenty seconds, and the AAP job
  takes about ten more. All three end *Successful*.
+
image::module-03/21-run-complete.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]
+
[TIP]
====
The *View job in AAP* link on the last row opens the actual job in
Ansible Automation Platform, with its full output. Automation
orchestrator did not reimplement anything: it launched a job template
and waited for it. This is great for when wanting to troubleshoot an
error you may have found with one of your AAP job templates.
====

. Switch to the *Mattermost* tab and open *Town Square*. Your card is
  there, with the agent's city as the title, its reason as the body and
  its fun fact underneath.
+
image::module-03/22-chatroom-card.png[title="", link=self, window=blank, width=100%]

== What You Learned

* A workflow is a *trigger plus steps*. The trigger is on the canvas
  with everything else, and `${trigger.<field>}` reaches its input from
  anywhere in the workflow.
* A *response schema* turns an agent from something that writes prose
  into something the next step can consume. The field names you choose
  there are the contract.
* *Run a step before you wire what comes after it.* Until a step has
  produced real output, automation orchestrator can only guess at its
  shape, and you are left typing expressions by hand. Afterwards it
  hands them to you.
* Automation orchestrator *decides*, Ansible Automation Platform
  *executes*. The agent chose a city; the job template that posted the
  card is a deterministic AAP job template that any of your existing
  automation could have been.
