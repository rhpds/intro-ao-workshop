= Introduction to Ansible Automation Orchestrator

== Introduction

=== What Is Automation Orchestrator?

Ansible Automation Orchestrator is an add-on to Red Hat Ansible
Automation Platform: a composable canvas for designing, deploying and
governing complex IT operations workflows.

A workflow is a sequence of connected *steps*. A trigger starts it,
steps do the work, and logic steps decide which step comes next. The
steps themselves are the pieces you already own. Every certified job
template, role and collection becomes a reusable workflow step
immediately, without modification. Alongside them sit approval gates,
REST API calls, and task agent steps backed by an AI model.

It does not replace the playbooks, job templates and automation you
already have. It connects them, and it does it inside Ansible
Automation Platform, under the same governance and the same control
plane.

=== Why Should You Care?

You have already automated the work. What has grown around it is
everything else: alerts, schedules, approvals, several teams, and a
dozen decisions about *when* that automation should actually run.

That orchestration layer tends to live in places nobody wants it:
glue scripts, runbooks, a wiki page, somebody's head. It is the part of
your automation that is hardest to share, hardest to audit, and
hardest to hand to another team.

Orchestrator makes that layer a first-class artifact: something you
design on a canvas, govern with RBAC and approval gates, export as
YAML, and commit to Git alongside everything else.

=== What Changes on the Canvas

*Triggers all live in one place.* Manual, scheduled, webhook and
Event-Driven Ansible triggers can all start the *same* workflow. A
monitoring alert and a nightly scan that find the same problem feed one
remediation workflow instead of two. And the finished workflow exports
as a single YAML file. Triggers, logic, approvals and job templates
all travel as one artifact, so it can go into Git and be handed to
another team.

*Logic steps route on real values, not just success or failure.* The
switch step reads a value from earlier in the workflow and takes the
first path that matches. Disk at 60% does nothing, at 75% cleans up, at
85% expands, at 95% gets a human involved. One metric, four paths.

*AI steps handle the reasoning, AAP still does the work.* A task agent
step can read an alert, a log dump or the text of an incident ticket,
summarize it, classify it and recommend what to run. Then an approval
step can put a human in front of that recommendation before Ansible
Automation Platform executes the job template you already trust. The
reasoning is non-deterministic; the change to your infrastructure is
not.

=== What You Will Build

You will wire up your own integrations and credentials, build a
workflow from an empty canvas, then complete three real scenarios
relating to disk utilization, incident ticket enrichment, and CVE
remediation. Every one of them ends in a live run you can watch step by
step.

== Who Will Benefit

This lab is for anyone who already automates with Ansible and is now
being asked to orchestrate around it:

* Automation engineers and platform teams who run AAP today and want to
  compose the job templates they already have into larger, shareable
  workflows
* SREs and operations teams wiring alerts, schedules and events into a
  single remediation path
* Anyone who has hit the limits of pass/fail routing and needs a
  workflow to branch on what the automation actually discovered
* Security and compliance teams looking at AI-assisted triage with a
  human approval step in front of the change

You do not need any prior Orchestrator experience. You should be
comfortable with what an Ansible playbook and an AAP job template are.
