= Introduction to Ansible Automation Orchestrator

== Introduction

Ansible Automation Platform is very good at running automation. You give
it a job template, it runs the playbook, you get a result. What it does
not do on its own is decide *which* automation to run, read an incident
and work out what it is about, or hold a change at an approval gate until
a human says go.

That is what Ansible Automation Orchestrator adds. An Orchestrator
workflow is a canvas of connected nodes: a trigger fires it, AI agents
reason over whatever the trigger brought in, switch nodes route on what
the agents concluded, and AAP job templates do the actual work. The
non-deterministic part — reading an alert, classifying it, summarizing it
— sits next to the deterministic part — patch this host, extend this
filesystem, post this message — in one picture.

In this lab you build that picture yourself. You will wire up your own
integrations and credentials, build a workflow from an empty canvas, then
complete three real scenarios that are shipped to you half-finished:
disk remediation, incident ticket enrichment, and CVE remediation. Every
one of them ends in a live run you can watch node by node.

== Who Will Benefit

This lab is for anyone who already automates with Ansible and is now
being asked to make that automation *decide* something:

* Automation engineers and platform teams who run AAP today and want to
  know where Orchestrator fits alongside it
* SREs and operations teams building event-driven remediation
* Security and compliance teams looking at AI-assisted triage with a
  human approval step in the path
* Anyone evaluating what "agentic automation" means in practice, with
  real job templates on the other end of it

You do not need any prior Orchestrator experience. You should be
comfortable with what an Ansible playbook and an AAP job template are.

== Requirements

Everything you need runs in this browser. There is nothing to install.

* The lab guide you are reading now, on the left

  Automation Platform, Ansible Automation Orchestrator, OpenFlake and
  Mattermost

[TIP]
====
Every module ends in a workflow that works. If one of yours does not,
you are not stuck — the *solutions* project in Automation Orchestrator
holds a complete, published, working copy of every workflow in this lab.
Open the solutions copy side by side with yours, compare the node you
are on, and carry on. Each module tells you exactly which workflow to
look at.
====
